`@kafkats/client` recovers from broker failures, shuts down within a bound, and sends and fetches with less waiting.

- Consumers and producers recover after a broker restart or a dropped connection. Brokers re-dial dropped connections with backoff, the fetch loop backs off instead of spinning and reports failures through the `error` event, and group rebalance steps retry while the cluster is unreachable.
- `consumer.stop()`, `producer.disconnect()` and `client.disconnect()` are bounded (about 5 seconds each) when a broker does not respond. Sends still in flight at that point are rejected.
- Multiple consumers on one `KafkaClient` no longer deadlock: each consumer group member uses a dedicated coordinator connection, and every consumer fetches on connections of its own, so consumers sharing a client no longer wait for each other's long polls.
- Producer: batches of one partition that queue up behind an in-flight request are merged (in order, up to `maxBatchBytes`), and records are appended in the order `send()` was called, also for sends that are not awaited one by one.
- Consumer: `runBatch` handlers get `ctx.resolveOffset(offset)` to make progress within a batch committable before the handler returns; `commitStartPosition(partitions)` commits the position partitions start reading from, before any of their records has been fetched; `position(topic, partition)` returns the offset the next fetch of an assigned partition starts at.
- Consumer groups: the leader starts a rebalance once the partition counts of the subscribed topics differ from those it assigned with, like the Java client. A consumer that joined a topic created moments before, while metadata did not show it yet, is assigned its partitions once it shows up instead of staying without any; added partitions are assigned after the next metadata refresh. The leader assigns and watches the topics of every member's subscription (a topic only other members subscribe to was never assigned), a deleted topic's consumed offsets are dropped instead of failing the rebalance, and JoinGroup v9 lets a static leader that rejoins a stable group keep watching (KIP-814).
- Transactional producers: a non-retriable InitProducerId or FindCoordinator answer (a transactional id the principal may not use) rejects at once with a `KafkaProtocolError` carrying its code, also for every later transaction, instead of retrying until `maxBlockMs` or throwing a plain `Error`; `disconnect()` stops an initialization that is still retrying, so a producer given up never bumps the epoch of its transactional id after a successor initialized. An initialization still retrying after `maxBlockMs`, also with a request in flight, rejects with a `TimeoutError` naming the transactional id, and the next transaction (of an idempotent producer: the next send) initializes again (like the Java client's `initTransactions()` after a timeout) instead of failing every later call, first awaiting an InitProducerId left unanswered, so that a late answer never bumps the epoch behind a newer request. Once an initialization gave up (`maxBlockMs` passed, `disconnect()`, or the `timeoutMs` of an abort that bumps the epoch), a coordinator lookup that answers afterwards sends no InitProducerId, which could fence a producer that replaced this one (one already sent may still be applied, like in the Java client).
- Transactional producers handle a failed epoch like the Java client. A fence (PRODUCER_FENCED on a produce, PRODUCER_FENCED or INVALID_PRODUCER_EPOCH from a coordinator) is fatal: the open transaction's `send()`, `sendOffsets()`, `commit()` and `abort()` (also once the handle ended), every later `beginTransaction()` and `transaction()` (also one run on a lane of a `transactionConcurrency` pool) reject with `ProducerFencedError` (breaking: a commit fenced by a successor rejected with `TransactionAbortedError`), and the producer never sends InitProducerId again, which used to make the coordinator abort the transaction its successor had open. An out-of-order sequence, a send whose outcome the retries left unknown and INVALID_PRODUCER_EPOCH on a produce no longer re-initialize the producer at once, which during the ongoing transaction fenced it for good: the transaction's sends are rejected (the others with `TransactionAbortedError`), nothing more goes out under the epoch, and its abort (also the one a `commit()` turns into) sends EndTxn and then InitProducerId to bump the epoch, so the producer runs the next transaction (an EndTxn answered PRODUCER_FENCED makes it fatal instead; `disconnect()`, also one called during the bump, skips it). The abort waits for the produce requests of the transaction, not only for its sends. An idempotent producer's send answered INVALID_PRODUCER_EPOCH rejects with a `KafkaProtocolError` carrying that code instead of a `ProducerFencedError`.
- Transactional producers keep what a transaction registered (partitions, offsets) with that transaction. An AddOffsetsToTxn answered after its transaction aborted made the next transaction commit the aborted one's offsets (input offsets advanced without their output); now a send or `sendOffsets()` still in progress when its transaction aborts (`abort()`, a commit turned into an abort, a timed-out `transaction()` callback, `disconnect()`) starts no request any more and rejects with `TransactionAbortedError`, the abort sending EndTxn once a request already sent was answered. `commit()` waits for `sendOffsets()` calls still in progress and commits their offsets instead of dropping them; a failed `sendOffsets()`, like a failed send, makes the commit abort, also one given group metadata of a member that has not joined. The abort rejects the records of its sends still queued behind another request to their partition (they were produced after the abort marker, into the next transaction), holds EndTxn back until no produce request of the transaction is in flight, sends EndTxn also when the answer to an AddPartitionsToTxn or AddOffsetsToTxn was lost (the coordinator may have begun the transaction), and rejects with `ProducerFencedError` when a fence arrives while it waits, instead of resetting the producer to `'idle'`. The handle's `signal` is aborted with a `TransactionAbortedError`.
- `consumer.committed({ timeoutMs, signal })` and `producer.partitionCount({ timeoutMs })` are bounded as a whole: an OffsetFetch or a coordinator lookup the broker does not answer no longer outlives `timeoutMs` (`committed()` then rejects with a `TimeoutError` whose cause is the last failure, also once retries used up the time), and neither sends anything after it. A rebalance whose fetch of the committed offsets runs out of `defaultApiTimeoutMs` fetches them again instead of failing the rebalance.
- `stop()` no longer cuts the group connection while the loop body of `stream()` is still working on a record; sends waiting for a topic whose partition lookup failed fail together with it.
